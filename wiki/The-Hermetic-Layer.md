# THE HERMETIC LAYER

*A page about a rumour. Nothing on it is a promise, and nothing on it says where to look.*

People who have read both of DAEMONS' [books](Books) sometimes talk about a third. They call it **THE HERMETIC LAYER**,
or just the spellbook. No two accounts of it agree on much. This page keeps what is said about it, and where the ideas
behind it come from -- because those, at least, are real.

## What is said of it

- **It does not come from the world it turns up in.** It is said to open on a warning: that some who read it went mad,
  and that anyone who wants their world to stay solid should stop on the first page.
- **It says what the rest of the game will not.** DAEMONS keeps one rule above the others: it never explains itself.
  This book, the story goes, is the one place that rule bends -- it says the quiet part aloud, in its own voice, and
  takes the jokes of CALLOW SCHOOL for the truth.
- **It treats words as spells.** It is supposed to teach **SPELLCRAFT**: a handful of spells, each leaning between
  reason and instinct, each with a cost that rhymes with what it teaches -- and one more that is not on the list.
- **It bends play.** Those who claim to carry it say the clock, the paths and even the scenes are not quite fixed for
  them any more, and that the world breathes in a colour between red and blue.
- **It stands on the other two.** It is said to take the GUIDE's reason and THE SUBTLE LAYER's noticing and make
  something of both.
- **Its author was a hatter**, and the people who mention him tap the side of their heads when they do. He is supposed
  to say he did not go mad. He says he went through.
- **It is the only book that glitches.** A page that shivers once, they say, and then holds still.

The few who say they have found it agree on two things only: it came to them after the other two, and it came late.

## Where the ideas come from

All of this is history, and a reader can follow every thread.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Hermes Trismegistus** | "Thrice-great Hermes", the Greek god Hermes joined with the Egyptian Thoth, god of writing and wisdom. Greek texts written in his name in the 2nd and 3rd centuries AD -- the **Hermetica**, the *Corpus Hermeticum* among them -- were translated into Latin by Marsilio Ficino in 1463 and shaped Renaissance magic. Anything sealed so well that nothing gets in is still called **hermetic**, after him. |
| **The Emerald Tablet** | A short, cryptic text said to have been carved by Hermes on a slab of emerald, known first from Arabic writings of the 8th or 9th century and in Latin from the 12th. Isaac Newton translated it. Its best-known line comes down to us as **"as above, so below"**: what happens in the small mirrors what happens in the large. |
| **Alchemy** | The search for the *prima materia*, the one thing everything is made of, worked through stages -- the blackening, the whitening, the yellowing and the reddening. Its master substance was **mercury**, quicksilver, which bears the name of Hermes' Roman self. |
| **Mad as a hatter** | Felt hats were made with mercury salts well into the 19th century, and hatters breathed it in. The tremors and the strangeness it caused gave English the phrase long before *Alice's Adventures in Wonderland* gave it a face. |
| **Grimoires** | Books of spells, such as the *Key of Solomon*, copied by hand in the late Middle Ages and the Renaissance. The word comes from the French *grammaire*, grammar: a spell was words put in their right order -- and English, by a happy accident, uses the same word for **spelling** one out. |
| **The witching hour** | "'Tis now the very witching time of night," says Hamlet, and for centuries that time was midnight. Newer folklore moved it later, into the small hours, when the house is asleep and the veil is said to be thinnest. |

DAEMONS adapts each of these to its own world, in its own words. Where the book is, how it is opened and what it does
when it is are left for the player to find.
