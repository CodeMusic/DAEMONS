# The world

What a player meets in DAEMONS beyond the battles: a world that keeps time, changes with the seasons and the weather,
and has things in it the game never points to. **Nothing in the game explains any of it**, so this page explains only
how it works, never where things are. Every new word in the game is still a **draft** until it is approved.

## The clock

DAEMONS reads a **cartridge clock** if there is one (an emulator's, or a flash cart's RTC switched on). Without one
the game still plays, and its days follow your play time: an hour of play is a day.

| | hours |
|---|---|
| **dawn** | 5 to 7 |
| **day** | 7 to 18 |
| **dusk** | 18 to 20 |
| **night** | 20 to 5 |

The light tints the outdoor maps as the day goes, and the **weekday** is on your USER card. The terminal in your
bedroom answers **TIME** with a CLOCK line when it is reading a clock.

## The seasons

The seasons **repaint the world**, and the two editions live in **two hemispheres**: CONTENT keeps a northern year and
CONTEXT a southern one, so in the same week one player's world is in spring and the other's in autumn.

The battles keep the calendar too: their backgrounds take the season, the hour, the weekday's colour and, on a
holiday, the holiday's own.

On fifteen of the early roads the grass changes with them: **one slot belongs to the season and one to the hour** --
dawn, dusk or night -- so some daemons are met only at a time of year, or a time of day.

## The weather

The weather follows the season, over the ordinary outdoor maps:

| season | the weather |
|---|---|
| spring | rain, and storms |
| summer | storms -- most days are clear |
| autumn | fog, and WIND |
| winter | snow |

It is drawn from the date, so it costs nothing in the save and two players on the same day in the same edition see
the same sky. A spell of weather can begin at any minute, last from ten minutes to a whole day, and cover one town or
both regions -- the islands can have their own. Most of the time the sky is clear.

Weather reaches into battle without touching the type chart. Each lifts its own routines, a quarter stronger:

| weather | lifts |
|---|---|
| storm | SIGNAL |
| fog | LATENT |
| wind | VECTOR |

Snow falls as hail, and the battle takes the weather's tint. Some daemons come out only in a weather, and the INDEX's
NATIVE line says so (IN RAIN).

## Holidays

The game keeps the real calendar's holidays, under the world's own names: **three a month** -- one shared by both
editions, one for CONTENT's northern year and one for CONTEXT's southern -- 36 in all. Playing on one changes the
game a little and never gates it: its own light and sky, some daemons turning up more on their day, fireworks, and a
gift, once a year -- sometimes an item, sometimes a line. On two of them, each edition's world lets in the other's
daemons, for that day only. The lines are kept in **THE YEAR**, a section of the NOTEBOOK.

**Every month has a colour and a note.** The months run the chromatic scale, January C to December B, from red
through green and blue to purple, the same colours as the days' notes. The day's banner and the USER card show them,
and the banner plays the month's note on the 1st. Without a clock, the holidays follow your play time.

## PLAY: the HARP and the FLUTE

Each edition has an instrument: **the HARP in CONTENT, the FLUTE in CONTEXT.** Take it out and a
**KEY WHEEL** of twelve keys opens, then folds into a **STAFF**:

| | |
|---|---|
| the notes of the scale | DOWN, LEFT, UP, RIGHT, B, A, SELECT -- one to seven |
| the octave | hold L or R |
| put it away | START |

Every note has its own colour -- C red, D orange, E yellow, F green, G blue, A indigo, B violet -- and stays bright
whatever the light. **Some things in the world listen** -- and to them the right notes in the wrong key are not quite
the same tune.

**Seven songs** are heard somewhere in the world. Play one back and it is learned, and each does something of its own.
What you play is written in the **SONGBOOK**, in the notes' colours.

## Colour is friendship

A daemon's colours fill as it comes to trust you: nearly grey with a hint of its type at first, half its colour by
the time it is friendly, the full ramp at the most. People stay in colour; some trainers' daemons never warm to anyone.

## The daemons, drawn again

Every daemon was redrawn for DAEMONS and coloured by its type, and since v11.316.7 every one has been **drawn again**,
in a style of their own: big, readable pixel portraits -- front, back and a party icon -- each with the one prop or
shape that says what it is. A daemon with other forms has each of them drawn too, its weathers or its letters. The
residents of one painted place keep the art they were painted in.

**The daemon at the head of your party walks behind you** as a small picture of its own, at the player's size.

## Feelings, never named

Your daemons feel things -- glad, cross, low, calm or afraid -- from what happens to them in play, and you see it in how
the one behind you looks and acts, and on a page of its summary. The game never names a feeling and never makes one a
status.

## Routines, signed

Every routine carries a signature of its own in battle: a few glyphs of bits and a three-note figure in its type's key.
On a holiday, the signature wears the month's colour.

## The map

The TOWN MAP keeps its shape, so finding your way is unchanged, but it is drawn in the game's own look: paper and ink,
routes like a score, and the sea a shuffle of bits and notes. The water in the world moves the same way. Some of the
buildings the story turns on have art of their own, drawn from the story rather than retinted.

## The GAME CORNER

The GAME CORNER's machines play as the companion's CASINO does: reels of the daemons you have seen, a coin and the
season's piece, a holiday's hats -- and luck from the game's own day: the holiday, the sky, the hour and the season.

## Music

Four towns have their own themes, and each of the hidden places has a loop of its own.

## The NOTEBOOK

The NOTEBOOK keeps the pages you find and write, in sections -- school notes, lab notes, letters, the SONGBOOK, THE
YEAR and more. A page not yet read carries a dot, and each says where it was found. A few end on a QR code, which
opens a song.

**The DREAM LOG.** After a milestone, at the next dawn, you say *"I had a dream."* -- and the dream is filed, as an
image, with a title and no interpretation. Someone else's dreams are kept there too. And a daemon carried on a watch
dreams while it sleeps there: at the next SYNC its dream comes home, and is filed under **ITS DREAMS** at the next dawn
-- titled, never explained (draft).

**THE LOG**, once you hold [THE SUBTLE LAYER](Books#the-subtle-layer-a-trainers-handbook), counts the reads and leans
you were given and which of them landed.

## CALLOW SCHOOL

CALLOW SCHOOL has a TEXTBOOK, an EXAM you can leave and come back to, and a DIPLOMA for passing it.

## The MARKS

There are eight MARKS, each won at a BENCHMARK, and each prints a **virtue over its shadow**. A MARK is a lesson as
much as a badge: a BENCHMARK's lesson is mechanical, so it cannot be skipped by grinding. Some of what the game
teaches arrives quietly and is never counted. A MARK and what it pairs with, once both are held, each say how the
other balances it; and when every pair is held, a last note arrives.

## The INDEX

The INDEX has **398 entries**, and every one is written twice -- the two editions say something different about each
daemon. Its area page says where a daemon is **NATIVE**: its region and, when it keeps to them, its edition, hours,
seasons and weathers.

**The cart alone earns the diploma.** Binding every daemon the cartridge can give completes the INDEX: the line that
grows only on the companion is a bonus, and so are the few daemons met just once.

## Places the game never points to

There are places in DAEMONS that no sign and no character mentions. Finding them is the game. Please keep what you
find out of issues and pull requests.

## SEND

A party daemon can be **SENT** out of the game to the companion, where your phone or a board hosts it -- and it comes
home with what it learned. While it is away, the people who tend daemons between battles sometimes hear how it is
doing: well, fine or quiet, never worse. A daemon can **evolve** while it is away, by the game's own rules, and the game
finishes the change when it comes home; one line of daemons grows only that way.

**Trading.** A daemon away can be traded with another player, or lent, through the companion. While it is on offer the
game will not call it home; one that arrives is written into your INDEX, says it was first met in a trade, and makes
a trade evolution on arrival -- unless it is only on loan: a borrowed daemon never evolves by trade, and cannot be
released or traded on. And one pair of daemons becomes something new only when the two are traded for each other.
See [The companion](The-companion).
