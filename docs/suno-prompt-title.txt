SUNO PROMPT - the front door
======================================================================

    C# major. I-vi-IV-V. 150 BPM. A fanfare that opens.

WHY C SHARP, AND NOT THE KEY OF THE TOWN YOU LEAVE FROM

7.14g first put this in C, because 7.4 gives Blanche C pentatonic and the
title screen was in the key of the town you leave from.

9.14 then established that THE FRONT DOOR IS NOT IN THE WORLD. That is why
colour could be spent there without breaking invariant 5 -- the title screen
is outside the fiction, so it obeys the author rather than the map. Which
frees the key to carry the other signature: SeeingSharp, the language, and
the pitch are the same word three times.

The cost is real and small. The door no longer sits in Blanche's C, so it
stops feeling like somewhere you have been -- and instead sits A SEMITONE
ABOVE everywhere you are about to go, which is what a door should promise.

WHAT WENT WRONG LAST TIME

The first attempt was correct and boring. It asked for "sparse, unhurried,
dry" and got exactly that, which is a lullaby, not a door.

7.14g's finding was that the tone was wrong -- and TONE IS NOT KEY ALONE. The
harmony was never the problem. The lift has to come from the arrangement.

THE OTHER CONSTRAINT IS STILL THE ENGINE

This gets transcribed to MIDI and played on THREE CHANNELS. Excitement has to
come from the writing -- register, rhythm, the shape of the line -- and not
from production, because production does not survive transcription.

A fanfare arranged for three voices is a real thing. A wall of sound is not.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
THE STYLE BOX
----------------------------------------------------------------------
instrumental video game title fanfare, C# major, 150 BPM, chord progression
C# - A#m - F# - G#, triumphant and inspiring, bold synth-brass lead with
bright bell doubling, driving eighth-note bass, energetic and uplifting,
late-90s handheld RPG main theme, adventurous, clean note attacks, steady
tempo, dry punchy mix, no vocals

----------------------------------------------------------------------
LYRICS BOX
----------------------------------------------------------------------
[Instrumental]

----------------------------------------------------------------------
THE ALLUSION, AND WHERE THE LINE IS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Ask for the CONVENTION, never the tune. The genre has a well-known shape and
that shape is not anyone's property:

    a held opening note that announces itself
    an ascending call
    an answer that goes HIGHER than the call did
    a cadence that lands on the tonic and immediately wants to go again

Do not name another game, franchise or composer in the prompt, and do not ask
it to sound "like" a specific theme. If what comes back is recognisably
somebody's melody, throw it away -- that is not a legal worry so much as the
whole point: this game has its own front door.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
AVOID
----------------------------------------------------------------------
no vocals or vocal chops       - there is no channel for them
no pads or ambient wash        - a sustained pad transcribes as one wrong note
no heavy reverb or delay       - tails smear the note onsets
no big drum kit                - percussion is the noise channel and gets
                                 dropped; the DRIVE must be in the bass line,
                                 so that when the drums vanish it still moves
no key change, no rubato       - the transcriber assumes one key, one tempo
no slow cinematic build        - a door does not have a first act

----------------------------------------------------------------------
THE SHAPE TO ASK FOR
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Sixty to ninety seconds. Fanfare, then the eight-bar hook, then the hook again
with the bass moving underneath it. End where it began so the loop point does
not announce itself.

The melody it replaces goes:

    C  E  G  B  |  C5 A  A  G  |  F  A  G  D  |  E  G  C  B

Rising, turning back, rising again -- a phrase that OPENS rather than
resolves. Keep that shape and let every note change. In C# it starts on C#.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
WHEN IT COMES BACK
----------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. Is it C# major at 150? Suno drifts, and this key is the whole pun.
  2. Can you hear THREE lines? Close your eyes and count. If you cannot
     separate the bass from the middle voice, the transcription will not.
  3. Mute the drums in your head. Does it still drive? If not, the groove is
     in the kit and the kit is the channel we do not have.
  4. Does the last bar want the first one back?

Then the transcription prompt, NO AUDIO gate first. 4.9 records what happens
otherwise: a model that could not hear handed back an earlier answer and
admitted in its own DOUBT field that it was a guess.
