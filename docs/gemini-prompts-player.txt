THE PLAYER — LOGIC and INTUITION
================================

9.10 replaced the gender question with one that does work:

    Now tell me. When the two disagree, which do you follow?
    LOGIC    INTUITION

    "The two figures are the same person's two ways of arriving at an answer,
     which is why they are deliberately not a matched pair of opposites.
     Neither is the careful one. Neither is the wild one. They are two
     people, and the player picks the one they would rather be."

SO THE TRAP IS THE OBVIOUS VERSION. Glasses-and-neat against messy-and-wild
makes one of them the boring choice and rebuilds the gender split with new
labels. Both must be competent, both appealing.

EVERYTHING IS HELD IDENTICAL EXCEPT STANCE AND GAZE. Same body, same build,
same clothes, same species, same age. One is ATTENDING, the other is
ANTICIPATING -- two ways of arriving, not two temperaments.

    LOGIC     gathered, weight even, looking AT what is in front of them
    INTUITION already moving, weight forward, looking slightly OFF-FRAME

Identical silhouette matters more than it sounds: nothing in the game refers
to either with a pronoun, and at 16x32 in the overworld stance is nearly all
the information there is.

ON THE SPECIES. Nothing in the game ever names it and nothing should. The
sprite is the whole statement. It is a primate -- upright, bright, hands --
because curiosity and working things out unaided is the job, and because the
mirror test sits quietly under a game about whether there is someone there.

    Write the FEATURES, never the species. Do not use the words monkey, ape,
    chimp or gorilla in the prompt: those words carry a caricature prior that
    has no place on the most-seen sprite in the game, and describing the
    animal directly avoids it entirely.

ON THE CLOTHES -- THIS IS ALREADY SETTLED AND WAS NEARLY GOT WRONG TWICE.
Section 8 rejected a first version that was "a cap and a strap -- Ash with a
satchel", and the brief that replaced it sits the player between three
archetypes: a scientist, a magician and a wizard. The resolution is that one
of them is free:

    A LAB COAT AND A WIZARD'S ROBE ARE THE SAME SILHOUETTE.

So the coat carries two of the three -- long, sleeved, reaching nearly to the
ground -- and the HAT only has to carry the third. A FLAT BRIM, WIDER THAN
THE SHOULDERS: it is the widest shape on the figure and the one thing no
other character has. A brim the same width as the shoulders stacks into a
mailbox, which is what the first attempt produced.

The design already exists as art: engine/gfx/title/player.png, 40x56, enlarged
to gfx/characters/player_reference.png. ATTACH IT -- BUT FOR CLOTHES ONLY.
Coat, brim, the strap across the body and the BOX at the hip come from there.
NOTHING ELSE DOES.

    THE FIGURE IN THE REFERENCE IS A HUMAN, AND THAT IS OUT OF DATE. It was
    drawn for the Game Boy build, before 9.4 settled that the cast are drawn
    as a fable. The character is an anthropomorphic PRIMATE as described
    above -- animal head, paw hands, tail, all of it. Take the coat, the hat,
    the strap and the box; take nothing about the species.

    THE REFERENCE IS TOO DARK, AND THAT IS HALF OF WHY THIS ENGINE CHANGED.
    Four grey tones gave that figure no way to light a face under a wide
    brim, so it came out shadowed -- a plague doctor, a gunslinger, a witch
    hunter. The GBA pivot bought colour and a wider design space, and the
    player is the sprite that most needed both.

So: same shapes, opposite tone. The coat is PALE CREAM, not black. The face
under the brim is FULLY LIT AND CLEARLY VISIBLE -- warm fur, bright eyes, no
shadow across the eyes, nothing hidden. This is a curious child in a working
coat, and they should look like someone you would be glad to be.

Save as gfx/characters/player_logic.jpeg and player_intuition.jpeg.
Generate LOGIC first, then ATTACH IT for INTUITION so the body matches.

Full-body character in the style of a Game Boy Advance RPG sprite. Flat cel
shading, thick dark outline, no gradients, no anti-aliasing. Plain flat
green background, no scenery, no shadow under the feet.

Subject: a young anthropomorphic PRIMATE of about eleven, drawn in the
classic cartoon funny-animal tradition -- animals through and through, built
from simple confident shapes, the style of Carl Barks or Disney's ROBIN HOOD.

Features: soft warm-grey fur, a rounded head with a gently domed brow, LARGE
bright curious eyes, small neat ears set close, a short flat face with NO
protruding muzzle and NO exaggerated jaw or lips. Slim, upright, standing
fully erect on two legs. Long expressive PAW hands with slender fingers. A
slim tail held in a relaxed curve.

Draw the face naturalistically and kindly, with honest proportions. Bright
and thoughtful, not comic, not goofy, not a caricature.

The character is DELIBERATELY AMBIGUOUS IN GENDER -- it should not be
possible to say whether this is a boy or a girl. No long hair, no eyelashes,
no skirt, no visible chest, no gendered styling of any kind. Just a kid.

DRAWN PLAINLY: no fur texture, no individual hairs, no tufts, no fluff, no
muscle definition. Flat solid colour with simple cel shading.

Clothes, from the attached reference: a WIDE FLAT-BRIMMED HAT in TAN LEATHER,
clearly DARKER than the coat, the brim wider than the shoulders and level all
the way round -- the widest shape on the figure.

    THE HAT MUST NOT BE THE SAME COLOUR AS THE COAT. A first attempt made
    both cream and they measured at identical luminance, so the brim -- the
    one shape no other character has, and what makes the player findable at
    16x32 -- dissolved into the coat. Tan hat, cream coat, clearly different
    values. A LONG PALE COAT, sleeved, open at the front, falling
nearly to the ankles, so that it reads as a lab coat and a wizard's robe at
the same time. Dark clothes beneath it, dark boots. A dark STRAP running
diagonally across the chest, and hanging at the hip a small grey MACHINE.

    ATTACH gfx/characters/box_reference.png AND COPY THAT OBJECT EXACTLY.

    Do not describe it from words -- two attempts did and both came back as a
    handheld game console, because "a small grey machine with a screen on a
    strap" IS a Game Boy to anything that has seen one. Listing what it is not
    only keeps the console in mind. The reference is the object as the game
    already draws it.

    What the reference shows: a CUBE, as wide as it is tall, standing on FOUR
    SHORT FEET. A dark inset panel high on the front. A seam across the lower
    half. No buttons, no controls, nothing to press. It is a tiny desktop
    computer -- something you set DOWN, never something you hold.

Worn and practical, not ceremonial, and NOT SINISTER. The coat is pale cream
-- cream rather than white, because the professor wears the white lab coat and
this character is not a scientist, only dressed in the silhouette of one --
and the clothes beneath it are warm mid-tones, never black. The face beneath
the brim is fully lit and open -- no shadow over the eyes, nothing obscured.
The overall impression is BRIGHT and curious. This is a child dressed in a
working coat, not a plague doctor and not a costume.

Full body, feet included, in a TALL frame. Under about eighteen colours.
