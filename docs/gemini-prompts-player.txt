THE PLAYER — LOGIC and INTUITION
================================

9.10 replaced the gender question with one that does work:

    Now tell me. When the two disagree, which do you follow?
    LOGIC    INTUITION

    "The two figures are the same person's two ways of arriving at an answer,
     which is why they are deliberately not a matched pair of opposites.
     Neither is the careful one. Neither is the wild one. They are two
     people, and the player picks the one they would rather be."

SO THE TRAP IS THE OBVIOUS VERSION. Glasses-and-neat against messy-and-wild
makes one of them the boring choice and rebuilds the gender split with new
labels. Both must be competent, both appealing.

EVERYTHING IS HELD IDENTICAL EXCEPT STANCE AND GAZE. Same body, same build,
same clothes, same species, same age. One is ATTENDING, the other is
ANTICIPATING -- two ways of arriving, not two temperaments.

    LOGIC     gathered, weight even, looking AT what is in front of them
    INTUITION already moving, weight forward, looking slightly OFF-FRAME

Identical silhouette matters more than it sounds: nothing in the game refers
to either with a pronoun, and at 16x32 in the overworld stance is nearly all
the information there is.

ON THE SPECIES. Nothing in the game ever names it and nothing should. The
sprite is the whole statement. It is a primate -- upright, bright, hands --
because curiosity and working things out unaided is the job, and because the
mirror test sits quietly under a game about whether there is someone there.

    Write the FEATURES, never the species. Do not use the words monkey, ape,
    chimp or gorilla in the prompt: those words carry a caricature prior that
    has no place on the most-seen sprite in the game, and describing the
    animal directly avoids it entirely.

Save as gfx/characters/player_logic.jpeg and player_intuition.jpeg.
Generate LOGIC first, then ATTACH IT for INTUITION so the body matches.

Full-body character in the style of a Game Boy Advance RPG sprite. Flat cel
shading, thick dark outline, no gradients, no anti-aliasing. Plain flat
green background, no scenery, no shadow under the feet.

Subject: a young anthropomorphic PRIMATE of about eleven, drawn in the
classic cartoon funny-animal tradition -- animals through and through, built
from simple confident shapes, the style of Carl Barks or Disney's ROBIN HOOD.

Features: soft warm-grey fur, a rounded head with a gently domed brow, LARGE
bright curious eyes, small neat ears set close, a short flat face with NO
protruding muzzle and NO exaggerated jaw or lips. Slim, upright, standing
fully erect on two legs. Long expressive PAW hands with slender fingers. A
slim tail held in a relaxed curve.

Draw the face naturalistically and kindly, with honest proportions. Bright
and thoughtful, not comic, not goofy, not a caricature.

The character is DELIBERATELY AMBIGUOUS IN GENDER -- it should not be
possible to say whether this is a boy or a girl. No long hair, no eyelashes,
no skirt, no visible chest, no gendered styling of any kind. Just a kid.

DRAWN PLAINLY: no fur texture, no individual hairs, no tufts, no fluff, no
muscle definition. Flat solid colour with simple cel shading.

Clothes: a plain dark red cap, a sleeveless olive travelling jacket over a
plain cream shirt, dark blue trousers, sturdy boots, and a small satchel on a
strap across the body. Practical, worn, unremarkable.

Full body, feet included, in a TALL frame. Under about eighteen colours.
