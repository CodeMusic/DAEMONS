GEMINI PROMPTS - the player
======================================================================

Five figures, eight files. Vanilla's player holds a Pokeball; ours holds a box,
and 1.3 is unambiguous about what a box is: a machine you offer a daemon as a
host, not a container you shut it in.

Do number 1 first and ATTACH IT to every one after. They are all the same
person, and a fresh conversation invents a different one.

Save each to the path named in its block, as .png or .jpeg either way.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE PLAYER, intro portrait  ->  gfx/player/red.png  (56x56)
----------------------------------------------------------------------

A young trainer standing three-quarters to the viewer, wearing a cap and a short jacket, one arm raised holding a small hard-edged electronic unit, roughly a cube and slightly wider than tall, with a narrow horizontal vent line across the front, one small round indicator light in a corner, and a port on the side. It is a machine, not a container - no seam around the middle, no button, nothing that looks like it opens. It should read as something you would ssh into: a little server, not a crate and not a dice-like cube. Confident, still, looking at the viewer. Draw it as flat illustration with a hard pure black (#000000) outline, 6 to 8 pixels thick, around the whole figure. Inside the outline use exactly four flat tones - pure black, dark grey, light grey, pure white - with hard edges between them, no gradients, no anti-aliasing, no glow, no colour, no texture. Plain pure-white background, nothing behind the figure, no ground shadow. Full body visible with a small even margin.

======================================================================
THE FOUR REMAINING FRAMES
======================================================================

Ask each of these in the same conversation as number 4, attaching it:

  Now draw the same figure again, in the same style, same outline weight,
  same four flat tones, same plain white background. This time: [X].

  gfx/intro/red_nidorino_2.png       the same side view, arm drawn back further behind the shoulder, weight shifted onto the back foot
  gfx/intro/red_nidorino_3.png       the same side view at the end of the throw, arm extended forward and empty, weight on the front foot
  gfx/player/shrink1.png             the same intro portrait, drawn noticeably smaller and narrower within the same frame, as if seen from further away
  gfx/player/shrink2.png             the same intro portrait, smaller again - about half the height of the original, centred in the frame

Two notes.

The back view (number 3) is the one that matters most - it is on screen for
every battle in the game, at 32x32. Favour the silhouette of the cap and the
shoulders over any detail; detail at that size becomes grey mush.

The unit is the whole ball-to-box redesign in one object. The failure mode is
a cardboard box or a dice-like cube: both say container, which is the reading
1.3 replaced.
