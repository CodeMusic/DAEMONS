GEMINI PROMPTS - the player
======================================================================

Vanilla's player holds a Pokeball; ours holds a box, and 1.3 is unambiguous
about what a box is: a machine you offer a daemon as a host, not a container
you shut it in.

WHAT IS ALREADY DRAWN, AND WHAT IS NOT
----------------------------------------------------------------------

The overworld sprite - the figure you look at all game - is DONE, and was
hand-drawn rather than generated. Sixteen pixels is one tile; a generated
figure loses its face, its silhouette and its walk cycle in the same
downsample that works fine at 40-56px. See tools/genplayer.py.

  DONE   gfx/sprites/red.png            16x96   six walking frames
  DONE   gfx/sprites/red_bike.png       16x96   the same six, provisional
  DONE   gfx/title/player.png           40x56   generated, --solid --fill
  todo   gfx/overworld/red_fish_*.png   16x8    three fishing half-frames

The fishing frames are hand-drawn too when their time comes, and are not
reachable in the slice. Do not ask Gemini for them.

THE CHARACTER, fixed by the overworld sprite. Every prompt below must match it:

  a WIDE FLAT-BRIMMED HAT, the brim clearly wider than the shoulders
  a LONG COAT that reaches the ground, sleeved, no legs visible
  a SATCHEL on a strap crossing the chest diagonally
  and the unit it carries

He sits between a scientist, a magician and a wizard, and the coat is what
does that: a lab coat and a wizard's robe are the same silhouette, so the hat
carries the third on its own. Not a cap. Not a pointed wizard hat.

The title figure is done and came from this pipeline. Note the --solid flag:
the title sprite is drawn as OAM, where the LIGHTEST level is transparent, so a
white lab coat renders as a hole. --solid floods the background at SOURCE
resolution -- where the outline is still six pixels thick and closed -- and
lifts any paper the flood cannot reach. Without it, 234 pixels of that coat
were see-through.

Do number 1 first and ATTACH IT to the two after. They are all the same
person, and a fresh conversation invents a different one.

Save each to the path named in its block, as .png or .jpeg either way, then
run `python3 tools/sprite.py` on it.

1. THE PLAYER, front portrait  ->  gfx/player/red.png  (56x56)
   Crystal's "this is you" screen, and the Hall of Fame.
----------------------------------------------------------------------

A figure standing three-quarters to the viewer in a long coat that reaches the ground, sleeved, with no legs visible below it, and a wide flat-brimmed hat whose brim is clearly wider than their shoulders. A satchel hangs at the hip on a strap that crosses the chest diagonally. They read as a scientist, a magician and a wizard at once - the coat is both a lab coat and a robe, and the hat is an alchemist's, not a pointed wizard's. One arm is raised holding a small hard-edged electronic unit, roughly a cube and slightly wider than tall, with a narrow horizontal vent line across the front, one small round indicator light in a corner, and a port on the side. It is a machine, not a container - no seam around the middle, no button, nothing that looks like it opens. It should read as something you would ssh into: a little server, not a crate and not a dice-like cube. Confident and good-humoured, looking at the viewer. Draw it as flat illustration with a hard pure black (#000000) outline, 6 to 8 pixels thick, around the whole figure. Inside the outline use exactly four flat tones - pure black, dark grey, light grey, pure white - with hard edges between them, no gradients, no anti-aliasing, no glow, no colour, no texture. Plain pure-white background, nothing behind the figure, no ground shadow. Full body visible with a small even margin. The face is FULLY VISIBLE and clearly lit. The hat brim is tilted slightly up and casts NO shadow across the eyes. The face must be one of the LIGHTEST areas of the whole figure, never the darkest. The expression is open, alert and good-humoured - someone pleased to be here. Not grim, not stern, not brooding, not mysterious, not a silhouette, not noir. Eyes clearly drawn and clearly readable.

======================================================================
BEFORE ANY OF THESE: THE FACE IS LIT
======================================================================

Craft rule 6: the game is funny in its ordinary moments and serious in three
or four of them. Corpus employees are cheerful. Scorn is genuinely warm and
never sneers. CAIRN is right and nothing undercuts him. Nobody in this design
is a monster, and the player is not a shadow.

Every prompt below therefore ends with the same paragraph, and it is not
optional:

  The face is FULLY VISIBLE and clearly lit. The hat brim is tilted slightly
  up and casts NO shadow across the eyes. The face must be one of the
  LIGHTEST areas of the whole figure, never the darkest. The expression is
  open, alert and good-humoured - someone pleased to be here. Not grim, not
  stern, not brooding, not mysterious, not a silhouette, not noir. Eyes
  clearly drawn and clearly readable.

A wide flat brim shadows a face by construction, and a generator asked for one
will put the face in darkness every time unless told not to. The first three
samples all came back shadowed and one had no face at all. That was a fault in
the prompt, not in the generator.

Check it after conversion: at 40x56 the face is about six pixels across. If
those six pixels are black, the character is a silhouette and it is wrong.

THE TITLE FIGURE  -  ask for samples, then convert
======================================================================

The hand-drawn 40x56 in the ROM is a placeholder and looks it. Replace it.

Ask for THREE variations in one conversation so there is something to choose
between, then convert the one that reads best:

    python3 tools/mksprite.py <saved>.png engine/gfx/title/player.png 40x56 \\
        --solid --fill --close
    make content && make context

mksprite now takes WxH as well as a single number. A daemon slot is square and
gets one number; the title figure is 40x56 and would be squashed by a square
crop, so give it the pair.

AND CHECK THE SILHOUETTE FOR HOLES

The outline pass blacks a cell only when the source line covers enough of it.
At the default 0.34 a cell the line merely grazes stays grey -- fine in the
middle of a figure, a visible BITE when it happens on the outer edge. The
shoulder of the first conversion had exactly that: one light pixel in the
middle of the black shoulder line, and it read as a clip taken out of the coat.

--close fixes it, and is the flag to reach for. Lowering --cover also closes
them, but globally -- it thickens every line in the figure to repair an edge.
--close fills only the gaps, and only where they touch the background, which is
the only place they show. On this figure it filled 8 and used LESS black than
--cover=0.26 did.

USE --fill, AND KNOW WHY

A standing person is about 0.43 wide-to-tall. The slot is 0.714. Keeping the
subject's honest proportions therefore fills 57% of the width and throws the
other 43% away as margin -- and at forty columns that 43% is resolution, not
whitespace. It is the difference between a readable strap and a grey smudge.

Vanilla's title figure fills 100% of the width, because it was drawn for the
slot. Ours is not, so --fill maps the subject's box onto the frame and scales
the axes independently. It makes the figure stockier. That is correct: every
Game Boy sprite of a person is stockier than a person.

WHAT MAKES A GOOD ONE

The hat brim and the hem are the two shapes that survive the shrink to 40x56.
Pick the sample where those two are widest and cleanest. Do NOT pick the one
with the most dramatically flared coat -- a very wide hem (0.93 aspect) gets
squeezed by --fill instead of stretched, and the flare turns into a grey
triangle with a person balanced on top of it. Faces do not survive
- do not choose on the face. If the unit in the hand turns into a grey blob at
this size that is fine; the strap and the brim are what carry the character.
