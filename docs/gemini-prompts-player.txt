GEMINI PROMPTS - the player
======================================================================

Vanilla's player holds a Pokeball; ours holds a box, and 1.3 is unambiguous
about what a box is: a machine you offer a daemon as a host, not a container
you shut it in.

WHAT IS ALREADY DRAWN, AND WHAT IS NOT
----------------------------------------------------------------------

The overworld sprite - the figure you look at all game - is DONE, and was
hand-drawn rather than generated. Sixteen pixels is one tile; a generated
figure loses its face, its silhouette and its walk cycle in the same
downsample that works fine at 40-56px. See tools/genplayer.py.

  DONE   gfx/sprites/red.png            16x96   six walking frames
  DONE   gfx/sprites/red_bike.png       16x96   the same six, provisional
  DONE   gfx/title/player.png           40x56   FIRST PASS, drawn -- replace it
  todo   gfx/overworld/red_fish_*.png   16x8    three fishing half-frames

The fishing frames are hand-drawn too when their time comes, and are not
reachable in the slice. Do not ask Gemini for them.

THE CHARACTER, fixed by the overworld sprite. Every prompt below must match it:

  a WIDE FLAT-BRIMMED HAT, the brim clearly wider than the shoulders
  a LONG COAT that reaches the ground, sleeved, no legs visible
  a SATCHEL on a strap crossing the chest diagonally
  and the unit it carries

He sits between a scientist, a magician and a wizard, and the coat is what
does that: a lab coat and a wizard's robe are the same silhouette, so the hat
carries the third on its own. Not a cap. Not a pointed wizard hat.

The title screen already has a first pass, drawn by hand at 40x56, and it is
blocky -- replace it with generated art through tools/sprite.py, which is
proven at this size. It is the second thing anyone sees.

Do number 1 first and ATTACH IT to the two after. They are all the same
person, and a fresh conversation invents a different one.

Save each to the path named in its block, as .png or .jpeg either way, then
run `python3 tools/sprite.py` on it.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE PLAYER, front portrait  ->  gfx/player/red.png  (56x56)
   Crystal's "this is you" screen, and the Hall of Fame.
----------------------------------------------------------------------

A figure standing three-quarters to the viewer in a long coat that reaches the ground, sleeved, with no legs visible below it, and a wide flat-brimmed hat whose brim is clearly wider than their shoulders. A satchel hangs at the hip on a strap that crosses the chest diagonally. They read as a scientist, a magician and a wizard at once - the coat is both a lab coat and a robe, and the hat is an alchemist's, not a pointed wizard's. One arm is raised holding a small hard-edged electronic unit, roughly a cube and slightly wider than tall, with a narrow horizontal vent line across the front, one small round indicator light in a corner, and a port on the side. It is a machine, not a container - no seam around the middle, no button, nothing that looks like it opens. It should read as something you would ssh into: a little server, not a crate and not a dice-like cube. Confident, still, looking at the viewer. Draw it as flat illustration with a hard pure black (#000000) outline, 6 to 8 pixels thick, around the whole figure. Inside the outline use exactly four flat tones - pure black, dark grey, light grey, pure white - with hard edges between them, no gradients, no anti-aliasing, no glow, no colour, no texture. Plain pure-white background, nothing behind the figure, no ground shadow. Full body visible with a small even margin.

Plain pure-white background, nothing behind the figure, no ground shadow. Full body visible with a small even margin.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE PLAYER, back view  ->  gfx/player/redb.png  (32x32)
   attach: the front portrait
----------------------------------------------------------------------

The same figure seen from behind and slightly below, as if standing just behind their shoulder - head, shoulders and upper back only, cropped at roughly the waist. The underside of the hat brim and the satchel sitting between the shoulder blades are the two readable shapes. One arm is drawn back at the side. Simpler and chunkier than the front view; this is seen small and constantly. Draw it as flat illustration with a hard pure black (#000000) outline, 6 to 8 pixels thick, around the whole figure. Inside the outline use exactly four flat tones - pure black, dark grey, light grey, pure white - with hard edges between them, no gradients, no anti-aliasing, no glow, no colour, no texture. Plain pure-white background, nothing behind the figure, no ground shadow. Full body visible with a small even margin.

THE TWO SHRINK FRAMES
======================================================================

Ask these in the same conversation as number 1, attaching it:

  Now draw the same figure again, in the same style, same outline weight,
  same four flat tones, same plain white background. This time: [X].

  gfx/player/shrink1.png   the same portrait, drawn noticeably smaller and narrower within the same frame, as if seen from further away
  gfx/player/shrink2.png   the same portrait, smaller again - about half the height of the original, centred in the frame

figure must stay centred and lose height evenly.

NOT THE PLAYER
======================================================================

gfx/intro/red_nidorino_1..3.png are CODEMUSAI, not the trainer - the intro
face-off is CODEMUSAI against CAREMUSAI and the player is not in it. An
earlier version of this file asked for a throwing pose at that path; it was
wrong, and following it would overwrite the intro.

The back view (number 3) is the one that matters most - it is on screen for
every battle in the game, at 32x32. Favour the silhouette of the cap and the
satchel over any detail; detail at that size becomes grey mush.
