## A -- THE THREE AT THE FIR (recommended)

*The whole parable, played. Three animals and the fir; what each does is the scene. Nothing is said about any of it.*

**The hare** is there once only, the first visit: it touches the fir, and the fir hums one bar in its key.

> Hare: Oh. Thank you.
> *(it hops away down the path; on every later visit there are only its tracks, leading out)*

**The magpie** sits beside a small cairn at the fir's foot, a pale stone on top. Every visit it is there.

> Magpie: It was warm, the day I found it.
> Magpie: I keep it here so it stays.

*Talk to it again:*

> Magpie: Touch it, if you like. Gently.

*Touching the stone (A on the cairn):*

> The stone is cold.

*With THE SUBTLE LAYER held, a read follows in the lean's purple (TOUCH, T-575's voice):*

> A read: this was held too long.

**The mole** is half out of a hole a few tiles off, a sheaf of pages beside it. Each visit the sheaf is thicker (three
sprites: a few pages, a pile, a heap).

> Mole: Leaf, leaf, leaf. North wind, two knots.
> Mole: I have nearly all of it.

*Later visits, one line each, in turn:*

> Mole: The light was here. I have it down.
> Mole: I hear it is beautiful. I have that down too.

**If the player PLAYs the fir's song in its key while the mole is there** (the instrument, vision 3.5), the fir answers as
it always does, and the mole stops writing for one line:

> Mole: ...
> Mole: Where was that? I had it written.

*And that is the end. Nobody answers the mole. The hare's tracks lead out of the grove.*

**Mechanical (craft rule 5)**: the only thing that changes the mole is the player listening, which is the fir's own
mechanic; the stone is cold whatever the player does. **Cost**: three animals' sprites (the mole's sheaf in three
states), one small cairn tile, one script, one TOUCH read line, one flag for the hare's single visit.

## B -- THE CARTOGRAPHER ALONE

*The lightest touch: only the mole, by a fir. The other two exist only as what the mole has written down.*

> Mole: A hare came. Touched it. Left.
> Mole: I have that down.
> Mole: A magpie took a stone. Still has it.
> Mole: I have that down as well.

*Later visits, as in A (the sheaf grows; "I hear it is beautiful. I have that down too."), and the same answer if the
player PLAYs the fir's song beside it.*

**Cost**: one animal, one script. **Loses**: Kael's cold stone, and with it the TOUCH read -- the scene becomes one of
three ways instead of the three.

## C -- A LOOSE PAGE, ITS LAST LINE TORN OFF

*The parable as a page in the NOTEBOOK's LOOSE PAGES (vision 4.35's RESEARCH NOTEBOOK; LOOSE PAGES 7, FOLDS, is the precedent), found
under the fir. Told with the three animals, in prose, five short paragraphs. The page ends where the post's last line
would be: the paper is torn there, and the NOTEBOOK files it as found.*

> A garden grew nowhere in particular. Its tree bore light instead of fruit.
>
> A hare touched it, said thank you, and went on.
>
> A magpie took a stone from under it, and built the stone a shelf, and told everyone what it meant. A fledgling
> touched the stone once. It was cold.
>
> A mole only felt it faintly, so it began to measure: every leaf, the wind, the angle of the light. It filled a great
> many pages.
>
> At the fire, the mole asked the hare if she had not wanted to understand it. The hare said
>
> *(torn)*

**Cost**: one page of text and its NOTEBOOK entry; no sprites. **Risk**: it is the closest of the three to *saying* -- the
story is told rather than happening, and a page that stops mid-sentence draws the eye to the missing line. It also makes
the parable LOOSE PAGES', which vision 4.35 keeps as CRYSTAL's own section; that would make it hers to have written down,
a choice in itself.

## Recommendation

the player listening (craft rule 5), and it connects to what is already built: the fir, PLAY and TOUCH. B is the cheap
version and loses the stone; C is the most faithful to the post and the nearest to breaking the rule the post itself
breaks.
