DAEMON SPRITES — the Game Boy art, redrawn for the GBA
======================================================

WHY THESE NEED REDOING. The 33 species we drew were drawn for a 40x40 or
48x48 Game Boy frame in FOUR TONES. Gen 3's frame is 64x64, and vision 9.4
refuses to upscale -- 40 to 64 is a 1.6x resample that softens every edge
7.14 spent weeks on -- so gbasprite.py centres each sprite at native size.
Correct, and the cost shows: PING occupies 19% of its frame, against 25% for
the vanilla creature it replaced.

So each one is REDRAWN at the larger size rather than resampled.

THE PALETTE, AND WHY IT IS SHAPED LIKE THIS. Gen 3 gives a sprite sixteen
colours where the Game Boy gave four, and 9.4 chose four because four was
what existed -- not because four was right. The scheme now is TYPE HUE
DOMINANT WITH A FEW ACCENTS:

    index 0        transparent
    indices 1-5    the TYPE ramp: highlight, light, mid, dark, near-black
    indices 6-10   up to five ACCENTS, keeping their own colour

The dominant hue is still the type, so the palette still reads as the chart
-- which is 9.4's actual argument, that colour carries the argument rather
than decorating it. The accents buy an eye, a beak, a marking.

TWO RULES THAT ARE NOT NEGOTIABLE.

  * THE BODY IS GREY, THE ACCENTS ARE SATURATED. gbasprite.py replaces every
    grey with the type's hue at that value, and leaves saturated pixels
    alone. So the body must be neutral greyscale with NO colour cast, and an
    accent must be strongly, obviously coloured -- anything in between reads
    as neither and comes out wrong. This keeps ONE drawing correct for any
    type, which is the whole point of the ramp being a parameter.
  * THE SILHOUETTE IS THE DESIGN. Attach the reference and keep the shape.
    These are not concept art; they are existing creatures being redrawn at a
    size that can hold more detail.

Save results in gfx/daemons/ as <name>_front.png and <name>_back.png.

==========================================================================
PING — front. ATTACH gfx/daemons/ping_reference.png (LEFT image)
==========================================================================
Redraw the creature on the LEFT of the attached image as pixel art, larger
and with more detail, keeping its shape and pose exactly.

It is a lean, swept-back bird built like a dart or an arrow -- narrow body,
long thin wings swept back hard, a sharp pointed beak, streamlined, caught
mid-flight moving to the viewer's left. Nothing about it is soft or round.

Draw the BODY in NEUTRAL GREYSCALE ONLY -- about five values from a near-
white highlight down to a near-black outline. No colour cast anywhere in the
body, no gradients, no anti-aliasing, hard pixel edges. Flat background.

Then give it AT MOST THREE small SATURATED ACCENTS: a coloured eye, the beak,
and one marking. These must be strongly coloured and unmistakably not grey.
Everything else stays neutral.

Make it FILL the square frame -- it should be substantially larger relative
to the canvas than the reference is, using the extra room for feather
separation, a defined eye, and structure in the wings. Same creature, same
silhouette, more resolution.

PING — back. ATTACH gfx/daemons/ping_reference.png (RIGHT image)
==========================================================================
Redraw the creature on the RIGHT of the attached image as pixel art, larger
and with more detail, keeping its shape and pose exactly.

This is the same dart-like bird seen FROM BEHIND and slightly above, as a
trainer would see their own creature: a compact vertical shape, pointed at
the top, wings folded back along the body, tail below. It is flying away
from the viewer.

Draw the BODY in NEUTRAL GREYSCALE ONLY -- about five values from a near-
white highlight down to a near-black outline. No colour cast anywhere in the
body, no gradients, no anti-aliasing, hard pixel edges. Flat background.

Then give it AT MOST TWO small SATURATED ACCENTS, matching the front view --

behind there is no eye and no beak, so the back carries fewer.

Make it FILL the square frame, using the extra room for feather separation
and structure along the back and wings. Same creature, same silhouette,
more resolution.
