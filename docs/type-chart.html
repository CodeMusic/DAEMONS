The Chart and the States

All 111 relations under our 17 names, what each one teaches, and how the argument survives a daemon that is two things at once. And, decided 2026-09-13: one rule for how every screen uses a type's colour, hues for the two types that never had one, and a mark on each move that says what it does.

Decided 2026-09-13, and built. The colour rule, the HARDENED and OPAQUE hues, the move marks and the unlabelled type line are in the ROM. What is still marked OPEN was not decided.

Colour: one hue per type, and one step of it for words

Every type keeps its one hue. ramp5 already turns that hue into five steps, and sprites use all five. Anything that is a word uses step 4: the ground under a type badge and a move name written on the white battle box. It is one colour doing both jobs, because contrast works the same in both directions.

That is the whole of the consistency rule. No hue changes for contrast: step 4 of all 17 is readable as text on white, and white is readable on it. The worst is FROZEN at 5.7:1; 4.5:1 is the usual minimum for body text, and the battle box's own grey text is 8.9:1.

What darkening costs

At sprite brightness 1 of 136 type pairs sit closer than 20 (CIE76), which this page already treats as the edge of telling two colours apart at 56px. At step 4 it is 27, and a 1-pixel letter is far thinner than a sprite. So a coloured move name tells you the family of its type, not the exact type — which is why the type name stays written in the info box. Colour to scan, the word to be sure. That also serves anyone who cannot see the difference at all.

DECIDED · step 4 Some words read as plain text, for two different reasons. A hue that sits near the battle box's own grey: CONTENT (11.0), LOGIC (13.2), LEGACY (6.9). A hue too dark and too weak to show at letter size: CORRUPT, LATENT, EMERGENT, OPAQUE — OCCLUDE and POLLUTE in the mockup are this kind. One alternative: words take the lightest step that still reads as text — the hue itself where it already clears 4.5:1 (CORRUPT, LATENT, FLOW, CONTEXT, EMERGENT, OPAQUE), and step 4 otherwise. That fixes CORRUPT, LATENT, EMERGENT, and leaves 20 pairs under 20 instead of 27. It cannot fix CONTENT, LOGIC, LEGACY, OPAQUE: those hues either sit beside the grey or carry almost no colour, at any step. For CONTENT that is the design — the thing itself should not look like anything — and for all of them the mark and the written type do the work. The cost: for the types it changes, a badge and a written name stop sharing one colour. Built at step 4; the alternative is one flag, tools/gbamovemenu.py --write --text-step lightest.

The two types with no colour

TYPE_COLOR has fifteen entries for 17 types. Today the badge tool gives HARDENED LEGACY's slot and OPAQUE LATENT's, and the sprite tool skips any daemon whose type has no entry, so those daemons still wear vanilla colours. Colour cannot be consistent anywhere until these two have one. Each option is measured against all the others; worst is its distance to whichever type it sits nearest.

The move menu: colour for type, a mark for what it does

A move answers two separate questions — what type is it, and what does it do — so it gets two separate channels. Colour already means type everywhere in this game (the rule at the top of the matrix: hue is the type, effectiveness is weight, never colour). What a move does is the same kind of thing as effectiveness, so it gets a shape.

Hitting is unmarked on purpose. 218 of 356 moves (61%) deal damage, it is the thing a player most wants to see plainly, and because every other class carries a mark, an unmarked name can only mean one thing. The classes are read off each move's effect and power in the build, not typed.

The marks are 4px. With one in front, every move name still fits its 64px slot (0 would not). The font's own arrows are 8px and 14 names would not fit — EXTRASENSORY, FRENZY PLANT, GRASSWHISTLE, HYDRO CANNON among them. 8 font cells nothing in the game uses — Ì Í Î Ï ì í î ï — get redrawn as the marks.

Dropping TYPE/. The label is 23px, and with it EMERGENT, HARDENED already run past the info line today. Without it the widest type name is 48px. Showing power there was considered: it does not fit beside the longest names at three digits, and the mark already answers whether a move hits.

A daemon that is two things

Both engines allow two types per species and multiply the two lookups, so a CONTEXT/LATENT daemon takes SWARM at ×2 for its CONTEXT half and ×½ for its LATENT half, and the answer is ×1. Four outcomes exist that single types cannot reach: ×4, ×¼, and either ×0 overriding everything.

That multiplication is not a mechanic bolted onto the thesis, it is the thesis at the scale of one creature. A dual type is two simultaneous accounts of the same process, which is what §0.4 says CONTENT and CONTEXT are. When both accounts agree that something is a weakness, it compounds to ×4 — two readings converging is not twice as much information, it is twice as much exposure.

PairingWhat the arithmetic says
CONTENT / CONTEXTThe thesis in one daemon: the literal thing and the frame it is read in. LOGIC hits ×2 on the CONTENT half and ×½ on the CONTEXT half — ×1, and the argument cancels itself out. Worth building on purpose.
CONTEXT / LATENTBoth halves take LATENT and CONTEXT at ×2 (§2.4's mutual pair), so it is ×4 to itself and to its own opposite. The most fragile thing in the game, against exactly the two types that understand it.
LOGIC / LEGACYRules running on old material. FLOW and GROWTH both ×2 on LEGACY, unmodified on LOGIC — a formalist that erodes.
STRATUM / VECTORContradictory by construction: STRATUM is the physical layer, VECTOR has no location. STRATUM's own ×0 against VECTOR does not protect it. A daemon that cannot be coherently described.

GAP §9.4 does not yet say what a dual-type daemon looks like. The type ramp is one hue across palette indices 1–5, and a two-typed creature has two claims on it. The cheapest answer uses machinery 9.4 already built: the ramp takes the primary type, and one of the five accent slots (6–10) is reserved for the secondary's hue — so the label stays unambiguous and the second reading is present as a marking rather than a wash. Not decided here.

Four of the fifteen shipped hues are not choices. §6 gives the Review Board its humours, and each already carried a type. The rest are claims, written down so a later argument has something to argue with — and two are proposals.

The two moves, 2026-09-08. Four pairs sat closer than ~20 in CIE76 on the mid tone — roughly where two colours stop being separable at 56px, and most of a sprite's area. They collided because their metaphors are adjacent: CORRUPT and STRATUM were both earth, SIGNAL and EMERGENT both electric-teal. Adjacent ideas make adjacent colours, which is the system working right up until it stops being readable. CORRUPT 10.8 → 25.9 and EMERGENT 10.6 → 26.0, each moved within its own metaphor rather than out of it, and only one of each pair moved.
