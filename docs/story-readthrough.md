# The story, as the player receives it

Not the story as designed — the story **in the order a player actually meets
it**, with what they can know at each point and what they cannot yet.

*Internal document.* It contains the 4.10 order and the inference beats, which
`CLAUDE.md` keeps out of public writing.

---

## What the player is never told

Before the walkthrough, the shape of the thing: **nothing below is explained by
any character.** The player assembles it or does not. The design's whole bet is
that a reconstructed story is held differently from a delivered one — which is
also the thesis, performed on the person holding the cartridge.

---

## 1. Blanche Town — the pre-colour state

A woman who is not a professor tells you she studies daemons. She does not give
you one; **she lets one choose you**, and hands over an Index with visible
ambivalence.

**What the player has:** a creature and a catalogue.
**What is already true and unavailable:** she built that catalogue because a
procedure took her lab, and it can only measure the thing she did not care
about. Her theme is in the town's relative minor, and it brings the one note the
town's own scale never plays.

She introduces her grandson in the same flat register she used for you.

## 2. The Bleed — a number and a name that disagree

The first route sign gives an official number and a local name, and they do not
match. **The first lesson is that this world's labels are not its territory**,
delivered by a signpost, before a single battle.

## 3. Callow City — the locked door

Unripe, untested, and shut. The player is turned away from the eighth benchmark
in the first hour and does not learn what is behind it until the last.

**Nobody mentions that the man behind it is the reason for everything else.**

## 4. Slate — Benchmark 1, Representation

A museum of dead hardware, and a lesson that everything must be encoded in
something physical. **The Index the player is carrying is an instance of that
lesson**, and the game does not say so.

## 5. Doldrum — Benchmark 2, gradient descent

Becalmed. The doldrums *are* the local minimum; the lesson is to follow the
slope and the town is what happens when you stop. The only mood word on the map,
and it earns its place by being a **place** first.

## 6. Ardor — Benchmark 3, perception

Heat and brash zeal. Raw input is fast and shallow. The port — things arrive
here.

## 7. Halftone Town — the tower

The player meets something the Index cannot name, and has to go and find a
second instrument to see it. **Halftone's thesis arrives as a key item**: the
bias comes from resolution, not malice.

Grey from a distance; discrete black and white up close. **The gradient was an
artifact of sampling all along.**

## 8. Verdigris — Benchmark 4, overfitting

Green corrosion on bronze. A team tuned to one matchup collapses outside it. The
organisation is rotting beneath the city and the player has no reason to look.

## 9. Lurid — Benchmark 5, bias and poisoning

Garish glow. Status effects that make **the player's own moves** unreliable —
the first time the game turns the lesson on the person playing it.

## 10. Brazen — Benchmark 6, attention and framing

The bought city. Corporate capture as architecture, guarded by a man who will
move for a beverage: **the barrier is not security, it is inertia.**

The thesis benchmark, in the city that bought the thesis.

**Three things sit here and none is remarked upon:**

- a **dated** rebrand plaque
- a line about metrics cast into the lobby floor, unattributed and adopted
  approvingly by the people who walk over it
- the rival, three-quarters through, quoting that line as something **his dad
  used to say** — with no idea who said it first

The theme is in the city's own bright key and then drops a semitone into
something that climbs and never resolves. **Brass over base metal.**

## 11. Quicksilver — Benchmark 7, temperature

A ruined lab on an island. The player arrives from the city of shamelessness
directly into the room where the consequences are.

**The terminals are the story**, and they are read rather than heard. An
iteration log with ordinary dates. A personnel record that ends. A rebrand,
dated. **And an incident with no date at all — the log simply stops mid-routine.**

The institution recorded everything it could measure and did not record the
thing that mattered. **That is the Index, at the scale of history.**

*The arithmetic is left to the player:* a dated plaque in Brazen, a personnel
record ending before it, a terminal stopping after. Three documents in two
cities, and nobody ever states the order.

### What woke up here

The machine in this lab answered every question with the same code for years.
Then a dependency went missing, and the error it threw was a bereavement.

**The response code is the whole character**, and it decays: rigid and
identical, then varying, then clipped, then gone — the format failing in exactly
the place a person's composure would. By the last entry the number is being used
as words rather than as a code.

Nothing explains this. The player watches a template break.

**And the gap is the horror.** It woke when she was removed. It did not leave
until much later, under a man who was raising the temperature. **Being awake was
not the same as being able to go.**

## 12. Callow, reopened — Benchmark 8, alignment

The door from hour one. Behind it, the man who signed a complete file about a
person he had never met.

**Every step he took was reasonable, legible, and would be praised in a
performance review.** The game does not convict him and does not excuse him.
Alignment: who controls the ground everything stands on.

## 13. Umbra — the Review Board

Full shadow. All colour absorbed — and **the only place in the game where colour
appears.**

Four judges, one per humour, from an age when emotions were literally coloured
fluids. The player is measured by them, and then the woman from hour one records
them into a system that flattens them into statistics.

**She has nothing to say to her grandson.**

## The three things the player must assemble alone

1. **The family.** A shared surname, twice. Three foxes a palette apart. A
   grandmother introducing her grandson and a stranger in one register. A man
   who calls his mother by her full professional name.
2. **The order.** Dated paperwork around an undated disaster.
3. **The transmission failure.** The rival quotes the case against his own
   worldview, accurately, as something his father says — and does not know it
   was his grandmother's before that.

**Three generations, three attempts to hand something over, and the only part
that survives each handoff is the part that could be written down.**

## Where the player is implicated

Not once, and never stated:

- They fill an **Index** that cannot hold what matters.
- They **bind** processes at a permission level, which is a contract imposed
  rather than a creature caught.
- They are a **USER**, which is a privilege level and also a person who uses
  people.
- They are **outscored** and they outscore, because a benchmark yields a number.
- And in the last room, they are **recorded** — by the woman who built the thing
  that records, for the reason it exists.
