CRYSTAL CLEAR — three treatments of the same character
======================================================

The point is to see them together and decide, so everything except the
treatment is held fixed: same person, same pose, same clothes, same framing.
Generate B and C; A is gfx/characters/crystal_speech.jpeg, already made.

The pose is fixed as her intro portrait -- standing, turned slightly, one
hand extended in a welcoming gesture, the other holding a notebook or tablet
against her side. Full body, tall frame.

Save as gfx/characters/style_b.jpeg and style_c.jpeg.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
A -- ANTHRO, BODY-FORWARD  (already generated, for reference)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
What we have: a golden fox woman, visible fur on face and arms, a full brush
tail, upright ears, a muzzle. The fur and the tail are prominent.

This is the version most likely to be read as a furry game -- not because
she is a fox, but because the BODY is the animal. Star Fox is anthropomorphic
too and is rarely read that way, because its characters are faces in
cockpits: a head, a flight suit, a job.

B -- HUMAN, WITH THE FABLE IN THE PRESENTATION
==========================================================================
Full-body character in the style of a Game Boy Advance RPG sprite. Flat cel
shading, thick dark outline, no gradients, no anti-aliasing. Plain flat
green background, no scenery, no shadow under the feet.

Subject: a HUMAN woman in her sixties, a senior research scientist. She is
entirely human -- no fur, no ears, no tail, no muzzle.

But everything about her presentation says FOX, without her being one:
golden-amber hair going silver at the temples, swept back to a point. A
sharp, narrow face with quick eyes. A long white lab coat worn open over a
purple shirt, with a high russet-red collar turned up. Lean, alert, poised
on the balls of her feet as though about to move. Warm, clever, and slightly
ahead of you.

Pose: standing, turned slightly to her right, one hand extended toward the
viewer in a welcoming gesture, the other holding a notebook against her
side. Full body, feet included, in a TALL frame. Under about twenty colours.

C -- ANTHRO, STAR FOX RESTRAINT
==========================================================================
Full-body character in the style of a Game Boy Advance RPG sprite. Flat cel
shading, thick dark outline, no gradients, no anti-aliasing. Plain flat
green background, no scenery, no shadow under the feet.

Subject: an anthropomorphic fox, a senior research scientist in her sixties.
Golden-amber, with silver at the temples.

IMPORTANT -- the animal is the FACE, not the body. Only the head reads as a
fox: a narrow muzzle, upright ears, sharp eyes. Everything below the neck is
COVERED. A long white lab coat over a purple shirt, sleeves to the wrist,
trousers to the ankle, proper shoes. NO visible fur anywhere on the body, no
bare arms, NO TAIL VISIBLE -- it is behind her or under the coat. Her build
is an ordinary human build under the clothes.

She reads as a scientist who happens to be a fox, not as a fox creature.

Pose: standing, turned slightly to her right, one hand extended toward the
viewer in a welcoming gesture, the other holding a notebook against her
side. Full body, feet included, in a TALL frame. Under about twenty colours.

THE BUG CATCHER TEST -- does C survive a character with nothing to hide behind
==========================================================================
Crystal is the easy case: a lab coat covers everything, so of course C works.
The vanilla BUG CATCHER is the hard one -- straw hat, white tank top, blue
shorts, sandals. Bare arms, bare legs, bare shoulders, and no coat.

So C's rule cannot be "the body is covered", because this class cannot be.
The rule is about HOW THE BODY IS DRAWN:

    the animal is the HEAD. The body is the SAME COLOUR as the head and
    drawn FLAT -- no fur texture, no pelt, no tail, human proportions.

The colour continues; only the TEXTURE stops. Getting this wrong produces the
uncanny result we got first time: a fur-coloured head on human-skin-coloured
arms reads as a MASK on a boy, not as a character. Star Fox does not do that
-- Fox McCloud's hands are fox-coloured, they are simply drawn flat.

If that holds here it is a style. If it only worked on Crystal it was a
lucky costume. Save as gfx/characters/style_c_bug.jpeg.

Full-body character in the style of a Game Boy Advance RPG sprite. Flat cel
shading, thick dark outline, no gradients, no anti-aliasing. Plain flat
green background, no scenery, no shadow under the feet.

Subject: a young BUG CATCHER, a boy of about twelve. He is an anthropomorphic
BEETLE-hunting kid whose HEAD is a mouse -- rounded ears, a short blunt
muzzle, bright eyes. Everything below the neck is an ORDINARY HUMAN BODY in
ordinary proportions.

CRITICAL, AND THE FIRST ATTEMPT GOT THIS WRONG: his bare arms and legs are
THE SAME TAN-BROWN COLOUR AS HIS FACE. NOT human skin colour. NOT pink. The
colour of his face continues unbroken down his arms, his hands, his legs and
his feet, because it is all the same creature.

That colour is laid down FLAT, with simple cel shading and nothing else: NO
fur texture, NO individual hairs, NO tufts, NO pelt markings, NO fluff at the
wrists or ankles. NO TAIL. The colour continues and only the texture stops.

He is a boy with an animal's head and an animal's colouring, drawn plainly --
not a costume, and not a furry creature.

Clothes: a straw sun hat with a blue band, a white sleeveless top, blue
shorts, sandals, a small blue satchel.

Pose: braced with feet apart, one arm raised high holding a butterfly net
above his head, leaning slightly forward, grinning. Full body, feet included,
in a square frame. Under about fifteen colours.

==========================================================================
D -- THE FUNNY-ANIMAL TRADITION, tested on the same BUG CATCHER
==========================================================================
C failed here and the failure was structural: an animal head on a human body
is a MASK, and no amount of colour-matching fixes the join. The Star Fox
argument does not survive either -- when those characters appear full length
they have tails, digitigrade legs, fur and paw-like hands. What is true of
Star Fox is true of its PORTRAITS.

The tradition that does full-body animals without reading as furry is the
FUNNY-ANIMAL one: Carl Barks' ducks, Disney's Robin Hood (1973), Redwall.

    the animality is INTEGRATED, not grafted -- paw-shaped hands, a small
    tail, a head proportioned to the body -- but drawn PLAINLY: no fur
    texture, no pelt, no physique, no fluff.

That is the difference from A, which had fur rendering and a big brush tail,
and from C, which had no animal below the neck at all. Same subject, same
pose, same clothes. Save as gfx/characters/style_d_bug.jpeg.

Full-body character in the style of a Game Boy Advance RPG sprite. Flat cel
shading, thick dark outline, no gradients, no anti-aliasing. Plain flat
green background, no scenery, no shadow under the feet.

Subject: a young MOUSE boy of about twelve, drawn in the classic cartoon
funny-animal tradition -- the style of Carl Barks' comics or Disney's ROBIN
HOOD, where the characters are animals through and through and are drawn with
simple confident shapes.

He is a mouse ALL OVER, not a boy wearing a mouse head. The same warm tan-
brown runs from his face down his arms, his hands, his legs and his feet,
because it is all one creature. Rounded ears, a short blunt muzzle, bright
eyes, a head slightly larger in proportion than a human child's. His hands
are simple rounded PAWS with thick fingers, not human hands. A thin mouse
tail, SHORT and unobtrusive.

DRAWN PLAINLY. No fur texture, no individual hairs, no tufts, no fluff at the
wrists or ankles, no pelt markings. No visible muscle definition. Simple
solid shapes with flat cel shading, the way a newspaper cartoon animal is
drawn. He should look like a character in a FABLE, not a realistic animal
and not a mascot costume.

Clothes: a straw sun hat with a blue band, a white sleeveless top, blue
shorts, sandals, a small blue satchel.

Pose: braced with feet apart, one arm raised high holding a butterfly net
above his head, leaning slightly forward, grinning. Full body, feet included,
in a square frame. Under about fifteen colours.

==========================================================================
D -- TESTED ON SCORN, which is the tone test rather than the join test
==========================================================================
The bug catcher proved D closes the join: paw hands, a head proportioned to
the body, a small tail, all drawn plainly. What it did NOT prove is that D
can carry an adult, because D came back CUTE -- Barks and ROBIN HOOD are the
priors and they pull that way hard.

Scorn is where that matters. 4.4 needs him genuinely persuasive: he is not
concealing anything, he is PLEASED with the arrangement and will explain it
to you warmly, and the player has to finish his benchmark unsure whether they
disagreed with him or merely out-argued him. A cuddly Scorn breaks that, and
so does a sneering one -- 3.1 keeps Brass City in reserve against exactly the
day Brazen starts reading as the game sneering at him.

And the tradition's most famous snake is SIR HISS, a sniveling comic sidekick,
which is precisely the failure to avoid.

The existing gfx/characters/scorn.jpeg is style C done well -- a cobra head on
a fully suited body, saved by the suit hiding every join and the hands being
red. D changes three things and holds the rest.
Save as gfx/characters/style_d_scorn.jpeg.

Full-body character in the style of a Game Boy Advance RPG sprite. Flat cel
shading, thick dark outline, no gradients, no anti-aliasing. Plain flat
green background, no scenery, no shadow under the feet.

Subject: an adult MALE COBRA, a senior executive in his fifties, drawn in the
classic cartoon funny-animal tradition -- animals through and through, built
from simple confident shapes.

HE IS NOT CUTE AND HE IS NOT COMIC. This is not a sniveling cartoon snake.
Draw him with ADULT proportions: a head SMALL in proportion to his body, not
large. Narrow, level, intelligent eyes -- not big round ones. Angular rather
than soft. He is composed, warm and completely at ease. He is the most
reasonable person in the room and he knows it. A faint, genuine, friendly
half-smile -- not a smirk, not a leer, nothing sinister.

He is a cobra ALL OVER, not a man in a snake head. Deep red, with a broad
cobra hood framing his face and a pale cream throat. His hands are simple,
rounded, three-fingered CLAWS, not human hands. A thick red tail emerges
below his jacket, resting on the ground behind him.

DRAWN PLAINLY. No scale texture, no individual scales, no glossy highlights,
no muscle definition. Flat solid colour with simple cel shading, the way a
newspaper cartoon animal is drawn.

Clothes: an immaculate grey three-piece suit, white shirt, red tie, polished
brown shoes. Expensive and understated.

Pose: standing straight and relaxed, one hand in his trouser pocket, the
other open in a small welcoming gesture, as though mid-sentence explaining
something reasonable. Full body, feet included, in a tall frame. Under about
eighteen colours.
